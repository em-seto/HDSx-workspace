# Plain-Language Summary

Understanding how health measures differ across income groups can help individuals in public health identify areas that may benefit from further investigation.

Using the classroom National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) dataset, this analysis examined average body mass index (BMI) among adults aged 20–80. BMI is a measure based on a person's weight relative to their height.

The results showed that average BMI was 29.4 in the low income group, 29.3 in the middle income group, and 28.6 in the high income group. Adults in the low and middle income groups therefore had slightly higher average BMI values than those in the high income group.

However, these findings should be interpreted with caution. The analysis did not account for the survey design, meaning the results may not accurately represent the broader US population. Missing information on income and BMI may also affect the findings. Additionally, the analysis did not determine whether the observed differences were statistically meaningful or whether income directly influences BMI.

These findings provide a starting point for discussing socioeconomic differences in health. Further research that accounts for other factors, such as age, and uses appropriate survey methods would help determine whether these patterns could inform future public health initiatives.
